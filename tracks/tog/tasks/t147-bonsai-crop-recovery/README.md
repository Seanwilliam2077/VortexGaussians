# An exact cropped scalar comparator on Bonsai

On the existing two-stroke Bonsai task the prefix-sharing method returned the same exact scalar outputs as the ordinary indexed method and took less median wall time in two independent browser processes. The paired median reductions were 5.5% and 1.8%. Each method used the same native crop and reused the static sorted scene upload for the gas-suppressed control at each fixed endpoint.

| Fresh process | Ordinary host median | Prefix-sharing host median | Ratio of medians | Median paired ratio | Median paired saving | Faster pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1,480.0 ms | 1,397.1 ms | 0.9440 | 0.9448 | 84.9 ms | 5/6 |
| 2 | 1,443.8 ms | 1,398.0 ms | 0.9683 | 0.9823 | 25.1 ms | 4/6 |

Each process ran an unscissored native reference and both optimized audits, warmed each method once, then timed six counterbalanced pairs. The second process started in reverse order. End-to-end process time was 38.38 and 38.50 seconds including shared preparation, correctness audits and cleanup. Shared ROI setup took 3.39 and 2.09 ms. Each per-action host measurement includes method-specific preparation, branch copies and restoration, guard, physics and rendering, synchronization, pixel readback and scoring. In process 1 the ordinary and prefix-sharing physics medians were 950.8 and 878.6 ms, and their image medians were 509.7 and 513.3 ms. In process 2 the corresponding physics medians were 923.5 and 870.0 ms, and image medians were 516.7 and 520.9 ms. Each phase median is computed separately.

The original frame was 1,560 × 1,040. Both observers kept the viewport and full draw ordering, scissored fragment work to 158 × 166 with a one-pixel halo, then read the inner 156 × 164 RGBA bytes. In each process, all four cropped images from each method matched every one of the 102,336 reference bytes per image. Native full-display hashes reproduced T141. The native-weight digest, source selection, physical endpoint, native successor and restored parent also matched. The two edits had distinct images and states; each returned zero changed mass over 411.0941503196955, so neither cleared the frozen threshold. The secondary camera had zero target weight and remained undefined.

This is a measured scalar-choice operation on one researcher-defined camera and one two-rejection decision. Setup is reusable for that fixed view. The comparison does not time delivering full pictures or playable continuation, demonstrate a choice preferred by an artist, isolate the speed of scissoring from its co-optimizations, or establish the best possible scalar evaluator. T144 directly measured low but nonzero contrast inside the target. An analytic bound strong enough to certify the two rejections before simulation would need its own validity and cost evidence.
