## Why the nonzero input can agree

With full coverage, the initialization sets each burnable texel's remaining fuel to a value between 0.75 and 1.25. An alight, dry texel subtracts the burn rate times the native adaptive substep from fuel. The other material channels do not read the fuel amount except for testing positive fuel or exhaustion. Neighbor ignition reads the Boolean alight predicate. Thus two different positive fuel values cannot alter ignition or spread through that check. Under common gas, zero wetting and spotting, the other three channels follow exactly the same update until a fuel value leaves the certified region. ## Exact material state at a requested fork

Use one native object trajectory. On a texel's first update that actually enters the fuel subtraction, retain its update index and its **stored** input fuel. Persist the ordered native dt sequence. At a requested output, copy the common catching, burnable/char flag and smoulder channels. Unlit texels retain the primary fuel. This construction needs a device rounding check and a whole-atlas byte comparison to independent sibling trajectories. Recording an unrounded output when its catching progress appears to hit one can be a step late because storing into `rgba16float` can round a value below one up to one. Subtracting a single cumulative real-valued burn duration also skips the intermediate texture roundings.

The work avoided is the sibling's complete material kernel, including gas sampling and neighbor lookup for still-catching texels. The extra work is event tracking, storage and lookup of the initial fuel and index, the sequence of dt values, and per-lit-texel scalar replay when a sibling atlas is demanded. Audit counters and synchronization used for evidence also cost time. This is an exact mathematical reconstruction candidate, with no measured speed advantage. An ordinary implementation can instead update just the sibling's one scalar fuel channel in the primary material kernel whenever a texel burns. This **fused scalar baseline** already eliminates the second full material kernel. Its cost is the scalar read/write during burning and its eventual full-atlas materialization.
