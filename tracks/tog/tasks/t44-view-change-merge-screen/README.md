## What changed the research decision

1. **A zero current-view error is not a high-order motion guarantee.** For a common initial footprint, moment matching cancels the first derivative of projected density error. Hidden image-plane/depth cross-covariance variation can produce a second-order error. Mean-only variation is generally third order; a symmetric mean pair gives a special fourth-order case. Those local orders are not finite-angle certificates, and squared L2 error has twice the corresponding norm order.

2. **Discarded detail matters, but keeping every child is not the only remedy.** Two positive Gaussian mixtures can have the same mass, mean, covariance, affine momentum state and entire initial optical-depth image, yet arbitrarily different later-view optical-depth point values. This refutes an exact/tight reconstruction claim from that retained state alone. It does not make RGB unbounded, prove perceptual failure, or prohibit useful conservative metadata.

3. **An inexpensive ordinary scalar survives the motion considered here.** A log-determinant KL upper bound can be computed from the original group in O(k) work and stored. Shared mass-preserving affine motion leaves it invariant; projection and Pinsker then give an integrated transmittance L1 bound for all orthographic views. It does not require framewise component access. A projected O(k) bound and exact O(k²) density overlaps are further ordinary options; binary-group checks are already fixed cost. Practical tightness and time savings were not measured.

The [author-hosted Runnalls manuscript](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2007/2797/content.pdf) establishes moment-merge KL bounds and discusses ordinary overlap metrics. The source screen used two targeted queries and four primary items, with no second batch. It did not establish that a matching modern graphics method is absent. The affine optical deductions and derivative expansions here are our algebra, not attributed experimental results from those sources.
