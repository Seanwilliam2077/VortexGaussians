# Next finite task: same-field ordinary-reference entry

T31 supplies a usable authored input, not a qualified renderer. The next useful technical step is to implement and qualify an ordinary entry on these saved fields before deciding that they require a new method. This note is a preparation boundary, not authorization to run an unspecified benchmark.

1. Read the frozen T31 field contract/carrier and the existing T22 ordinary integrator/dispatcher contract. Reuse available analytic Gaussian cumulative mass and Gaussian-aware interval integration; do not treat a naive all-pairs scalar loop as the only strong baseline. No new external source discovery, package installation, capture, asset, training or scene is needed for this entry.
2. Define a unit-ray adapter for the exact stored positions/h/k/colors, all three original cameras and the stated pixel convention/clip planes. Keep the original full untruncated field as the target. Specify conservative spatial pruning and a complete error account for HDR radiance, including downstream effects of omitted extinction. Empty rays, near/far clipping and Gaussian tails must not silently change the model.
3. Before numerical execution, freeze one bounded correctness protocol: exact input hashes, deterministic geometric ray coverage independent of output/error, ordinary reference and comparison rules, accuracy targets, work/time cap, failure/stop rule and all reported costs. Cover both fixed times and all three views without choosing only bright or favorable pixels. Scope may be a declared fixed qualification subset; do not claim that subset establishes complete-frame accuracy or speed.
4. Qualification must establish the mathematical conversion and ordinary-output agreement, with independent reference/conservation evidence adequate to the claimed tolerance. T19's failed Simpson estimator stays failed; local estimator differences alone are not an unconditional certificate. T22's prior48 normalized-color fixtures do not automatically qualify this HDR workload. Review saved outputs without re-running to erase failures.
5. Only after that entry passes should a complete-frame quality/cost protocol be prepared. Charge full camera, spatial-index preparation, traversal, pruning budgets, failed shortcuts, integration, upload/synchronization/readback and conversion. Separate simulation cost. Define a practical quality/cost objective BEFORE timing, and report whether the ordinary method already meets it. No new candidate method is selected or queued here.

This progression can expose a worthwhile problem or show that no useful gap exists on the existing local workload. Neither outcome establishes TOG novelty. Do not make a denser/new case just to force a gap, relabel a model mismatch as an improvement, claim multiple scenes from the three cameras, or use lack of a bottleneck here to conclude that all ordinary methods are universally sufficient. Broader workload and externally meaningful task evidence remain future requirements for a paper.
