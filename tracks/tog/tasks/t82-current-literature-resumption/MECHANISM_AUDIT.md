## Falsifiable candidate

Let S be a complete particle/material state, Phi_tau its short-time evolution under a declared model, and H the source-to-material heat response. A proposed reduced state C(S) should approximate R_tau(S)=H(Phi_tau(S)), not merely instantaneous moments and H(S). A diagnostic must predict from the common pre-B state; using the held-out actual future to choose particles would leak the answer.

For native evolution, S must also include or reconstruct the required particle ordering, clocks, layer seeds, conditional RNG state, controls and scheduled emission inputs. A complete particle export alone is not assumed to be a restartable simulation state.

This becomes interesting only if ordinary allocation leaves a worthwhile capacity/cost conflict. Appending predicted response samples to an NNLS matrix is ordinary space-time quadrature/feature fitting. A potentially more substantive question is whether a cheap, checkable error bound can certify when a reduced response cannot change a near-term ignition decision, and trigger retention of full state otherwise. Neither the bound, its cheapness, nor novelty is established here. Ordinary binned moment/rate fitting with warm starts is required, not a deliberately weak comparison.

## Native-operator incompatibilities

The unchanged demo has no particle multiplicity or physical volume weight. Four source facts preclude treating a generic positive NNLS coefficient as a drop-in weight:

1. Material flame heating selects every seventh array entry per layer, requires T>=0.55, and stops after 160 hot samples (`demo/index.html`, around line1145). Deleting particles changes the selected identities even with an unchanged reconstructed field. T81 uses this path.
2. VPM velocity uses signed G; dynamic Tb is a Shepard ratio with a self contribution, and its gradient uses a moment normalization. Multiplicity must weight both numerator and denominator; multiplying an intensive temperature is incorrect (around lines1931,2095).
3. The director uses |G|+1e-4 centroids and unweighted temperature/count statistics. Baroclinic G increments and the +/-60 clamp are per particle. A representation of several copies needs consistent extensive source and clamp semantics, not just a changed initial G (around lines1958,2114).
4. Rendering density d changes footprint and clamped nonlinear alpha; T and random phase affect color. Multiplying d does not combine optical mass. Even coincident equal-color/footprint copies need transmittance composition, and that special case does not define the general native reduction operator (around lines420,2716).

These observations constrain this code, not all particle methods. T81's complete recorded particles do not make a turnkey compression benchmark. A heat-query reduction could retain all particle dynamics, but then it must not claim a particle-capacity reduction. Full-state reduction requires an explicit weighted model and a multiplicity-one regression; replacing legacy sampling with an all-particle heating reference is a disclosed model change, not a free fix.
