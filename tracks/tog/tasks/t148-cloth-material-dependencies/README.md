# Three native cloth controls did not qualify a new gas-sharing experiment

Blackbody allows an artist to change a fabric's weight, treat it as flame-retardant, or start it wet. Each operation has a direct path to the gas simulation. The most promising distinction is between a fabric heating below ignition and one actually emitting fuel. An ignition threshold can remain dormant for many updates. During that period the differing coefficient alone does not establish different *evolving* cloth states. Once a vertex burns on one branch, its fuel-deposition path is active at the next cloth-to-gas transfer. The source screen found no inexpensive prospective certificate for two further whole-gas updates after the registered scheduling lag. No simulation or candidate search was run. | Native edit | First relevant physical dependencies | Consequence for a shared-gas claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry fabric weight | `Weight` scales surface density, vertex inverse mass and the dry heating time constant. Mass affects drag acceleration and the impulse returned to the air. Burning fuel production also uses surface density. | A difference in cloth temperature could precede ignition, but equal motion, normals and drag would still have to be proved on the actual unpinned vertices. |
| Fire retardancy | The UI gives 0.3 as an example flammability multiplier. The same coefficient divides the ignition threshold and multiplies fuel production, which also determines smoke. | A later first ignition is a meaningful edit. Whenever the complete dynamic state is still the same, ordinary replay can reuse it. When the burning flags separate, the next deposition can differ. |
| Initial wetness | Water is stored per vertex, changes over time, adds carried mass and heat capacity, and can suppress ignition. Wet cloth also enables water and steam handling. | This yields an actual material-state difference from initialization. It also changes mechanical and gas-reaction inputs. A comparison of temperature or ignition flags alone misses these paths. |

**Dry heat capacity.** This fabric solver sets a single dry specific heat of 1,300 J/(kg K). Its material upload computes the heating time constant from surface density times that constant divided by twice the per-face heat-transfer coefficient. The separate rigid-object material table has a heat-capacity field, including an upholstery entry. This table does not provide the cloth simulator with an independent dry-specific-heat knob. A cloth preset can change several physical coefficients at once. Pinning the edges of a panel leaves interior degrees of freedom, and the released flag is held along one side. **What the retardant edits.** In the end-of-substep shader, the dry heating recurrence uses the local gas, the previous burning flag, thermal time constant and heat from neighbors. The flammability coefficient appears in the ignition/extinction test. The next coupling splat tests the burning flag, deposits weighted fuel and smoke, and uses a maximum for heat. Thus the fact that the coupling shader does not directly read a vertex's continuous temperature does not by itself give post-divergence reuse for this material control. Render material data also carries the ignition threshold, so a static shader argument or image difference cannot certify divergent physical cloth. The runtime can dispatch an additional radiant heating stage, which was not certified as an alternate edit path in this bounded screen. The registration called the released shot a “burning flag.” That wording was inaccurate. T137 has an upwind fire and fabric combustion disabled. It supplies a stiffness control, not a measured retardant transition. **Why moisture needs a different proof.** The predictor accounts for water in the moving mass and drag. The thermal shader evolves evaporation and burning, and the runtime sets a gas-reaction flag when cloth may be wet. It dispatches steam hooks for the coarse and optional fine scalar fields. Two different nonzero wetness values can set the same Boolean and still produce different drag impulses or steam production. Equal wet/ready flags would therefore be only part of a proof. **The ordinary control.** Blackbody already distinguishes appearance-only settings such as weather sparkle through `LOOK_KEYS`. Such edits can reuse complete simulation. For the physical edits above, an ordinary lockstep method can advance both cloth branches, construct their pending coupling buffers, compare all 13 integer channels exactly, and run one gas update when the other gas inputs, flags, scheduling and initial gas also match. A robust implementation would charge construction of both cloth states and splats, comparison or verification of hashes, synchronization, forks, and fallback. Truncation at each floating-point contribution and the max reduction allow real-valued differences to vanish. They do not show that any registered edit conserves every consumed output for multiple gas updates. The existing axis predicate can also recognize some differing buffers with equal gas effect. On the published ordinary flag pair, the first divergent material step already changed feedback; identical replay reproduced 121 pending grids. The remaining opening is specific. A future native material operation would need a physical degree of freedom that genuinely evolves differently while its mechanical and emission inputs remain equal by a check cheaper than advancing both branches and comparing their pending feedback. The present screen establishes dependencies and missing evidence for three controls. It cannot rule out coincident feedback, a different operation, or a profitable ordinary exact-input cache. No new numerical experiment is admitted here.
