# Does a dynamic cold-receiver spatial index change the cost of exact edit queries?

Development-only study after T114. In its Firewoods B→A 72-frame reconstruction, 6,829 gas samples visited 2,824,238 geometrically in-support sites and wrote heat to 367,383 actually cold receivers. Both the ordinary and deferred implementations still enumerated those contacts in the unfiltered native spatial hash. Test a shared monotone receiver index on the same known two Firewoods camera-0 quarter-matte edits (A→B and primary B→A, T108 readout, H=180). No future events or new sites/cameras are chosen. Build a hashed or dense bounded uniform grid at actual ignition radius. Initially insert only cold sites; immediately detach a site at its native cold-to-burning update. For a projected branch index its known monitored cold sites only; for ordinary, off-region repair and fallback index all currently cold sites. The full backend gets the same implementation, cold geometry preparation opportunity, persistent static cell mapping and within-branch mutable state as the candidate. Charge per-branch rebuild/removal, geometry cost and memory; restore or discard on rewind. Native heat is deposited into all state==0 sites (including any cold sites with zero fuel), and their threshold crossing still requires positive fuel. No physical controls, filters, emission decisions, radius or numeric stores change.

Cross three exact query schedules (ordinary complete active replay, dense chronological logged repair, T114 temporal sitewise repair) and two exact flame lookup modes (original full hash vs evolving cold grid). In fixed initial order ordinary, repair, sitewise, ordinary-cold, repair-cold, sitewise-cold. One excluded warm-up and six same-state measured repetitions per method and edit with rotating order; each method occupies each within-wave ordinal once. Charge actual click, query batching, grid construction and dispatch/restore in host-delivered latency. Compare raw cases, paired within-wave signs and ranges, and warm and first-use costs. Audit complete 180-frame curves, all registered physical boundary and next-native-step fingerprints; separate direct state equality for the cold implementation where feasible. Count builds, removals, broad and true-radius visits, active density and typed bytes in a separate profile pass; no profiled loop instrumentation in official warm comparisons. Verify historical full trajectories as in T108.

The primary attribution is whether either deferred cold route is at least 10% below independently optimized ordinary-cold on B→A and wins at least five of the six matched waves. Study each deferred mode individually rather than taking a different winner per wave. A short A→B check tests the secondary direction. Record when ordinary replay benefits as much, baseline ranking changes or measurements are ambiguous. Stop this development study without running another variant if neither deferred schedule meets the primary gate. If one qualifies, only then consider preregistered transfer on a distinct previously unexamined scene/editor condition. This two-edit stage does not claim a held-out or population result.
