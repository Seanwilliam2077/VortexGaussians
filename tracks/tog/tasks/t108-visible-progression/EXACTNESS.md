# Why a region can contain multiple new burners inside one certified prefix

Fix the native operator, timestep, sorted gas particles, graph-service cap, emission quota and the T95 boundary conditions. At this block's entry let the ordered burning frontier be F, its length n, the emitter cursor c and its offered source count per step b. The existing T100 guard chooses a length L for which every member of F survives all L fuel updates, n is at least both service caps, and c + Lb < n. All newly ignited sites, including regional ones, append after the old surviving frontier. Removing later additions cannot change the first serviced graph donors. No admitted emitter cursor reaches those additions during the block. Gas follows its full native trajectory when the ordered source offers, capacity handling and RNG stream match. This applies to the current stable-index, monotone-ignition, elapsed-clock fire operator. An emission rule weighted by aggregate burned area would require a different argument.

Consider a material site that was cold and fuel-positive at the second stroke. Before its first ignition, its heat at each scan receives the same ordered admitted hot-carrier contributions, binary32 decay, and graph contributions from the same serviced donors. The projected update therefore crosses the native threshold on the same frame. Once the native scan changes the site's state, set its local status to burned. Future projected flame deposits and cached graph edges both check that this local status is still cold before updating heat. A later local char transition would neither make it cold again nor place it inside the old serviced prefix. The implementation keeps a private monotone bit for that purpose. A region may therefore produce many new local burners before expiry; none becomes a donor or an emitter inside this certificate. No partial frontier is available for ordinary continuation.

Assign each originally eligible site a fixed nonnegative integer opacity contribution. Add the weight only on the unique cold-to-burning transition and finish the scan for *all* sites in that frame. The horizon test then operates on the exact native regional count and integer sum, including ties. Hidden and rounded-to-zero sites still enter the regional count. A zero total yields an undefined quarter rather than an immediate crossing.

At a repair boundary restore the saved full material at block entry. Replay each saved, ordered gas deposit and the entire native material scan and graph/fuel update, using the recorded emitter cursor to avoid running the same emission twice. The retained gas, draw counter and native simulation clock are already at the boundary. Reconciliation does not call the regional counter. Its accumulated count and mass already contain this prefix. A subsequent block rebuilds its cold list from this full repaired state. The restart control discards its speculative material and its accumulated result, then recomputes from the post-click input on the complete branch. Connecting either branch to an arbitrary alternate scheduler requires its own service proof.

For a returned threshold crossing, all three methods stop after the final complete set of same-frame regional onsets has been counted. Once this result is determined the disposable query may omit the material tail and feed. The full-deadline diagnostic keeps those operations, compares the entire mass and count trajectory with the independently captured native ledger, and checks complete material, full particle records, frontier, cursor, clock and random-stream markers at every repaired boundary against the ordinary branch. Full-state fingerprints use one numerical hash and an independent native marker, with the serialized length also recorded. Agreement on the tested inputs supports the implementation; generality comes from the guard's assumptions.
