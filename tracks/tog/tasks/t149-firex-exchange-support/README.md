# A finite distance through solid wood can shield an edited gas trajectory

Fire-X GS has an independent mechanism that the earlier examination of surface heat exchange missed. Its optional solid conduction stage reads the preceding temperatures of face-neighbor wood cells. Only wood adjacent to in-domain open gas exchanges sensible energy or emits fuel. A change buried inside connected wood can therefore produce genuinely different temperatures while all gas-side inputs remain equal for a bounded number of complete steps. The bound comes from distance through the occupied wood. It does not require comparing two full gas states. No native Fire-X trajectories or editing times were measured in this screen. ### The conditional certificate

Let M contain every wood voxel whose initial temperature differs after both physical candidate brushes. The original localized ignition kernel paints a sphere in world space and takes the maximum with the existing wood temperature. Equal initialization of open gas also needs checking because the seed kernel uses the maximum adjacent wood temperature, thresholds it at 450 K, and caps it at 750 K. A strictly buried M cannot change this seed. The pair must agree on geometry, fuel mass, all other fields, forcing and schedule. An empty M leaves both physical initial states equal and is an ordinary reuse case. [Shipped brush and seed](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/wood_thermal.py#L18-L57) · [Original reset and launch order](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/wood_thermal.py#L91-L156)

On the fixed wood mask, compute the shortest six-neighbor distance D from M to the set of wood voxels adjacent to open in-domain gas. Use complete wood connectivity as a conservative superset if material later vanishes. One native ping-pong diffusion substep moves thermal dependence by at most one edge. While the cumulative number of diffusion substeps is strictly less than D, all exposed wood temperatures and masses agree after diffusion. Interior sites independently return from wood exchange when they have no exposed face. Equal exposed materials and equal incoming gas yield the same **per-face contributions** to gas energy and fuel. The next complete gas evolution is therefore shared for a deterministic executor with the same reduction order. The solver first advances velocity, pressure and gas transport, then conducts wood heat, applies surface exchange and performs the gas reaction. Neither the apparent global reach of the pressure solve nor a camera framebuffer changes the distance argument. [Diffusion count and ping-pong](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/wood_thermal.py#L141-L156) · [Coupled native schedule](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/solver.py#L622-L735)

For q positive diffusion substeps per gas step, this gives at least max(0, ceil(D/q) − 1) complete steps, capped at the requested horizon. Crossing the bound invalidates the certificate; gas can remain equal longer due to quantization, inactive sources, or weak heating. With conductivity zero or an isolated wood component, the whole horizon may be independent and ordinary gas caching can use the same observation. The proof here covers dry fire evolution without an additional material-reading water update; the native command performs a separate Water.step during displayed frames. A dry implementation must audit its actual state and any other feedback hooks. Concurrent floating additions to the gas and cumulative floating ledgers also require an ordinary A/A repeat on the exact device. Structural equality of contribution lists does not guarantee a particular GPU ordering or identical bits across fresh runs. [Atomic gas and ledger writes](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/solver.py#L428-L481) · [Native dry control, warm-up and displayed schedule](https://github.com/zju3dv/PhysStudio/blob/3e084fe7a71eface1cf42720a9fe896c993b967e/firex_gs/simulate.py#L45-L111)

### Four checks at a released physical scale

The probe uses a nine-voxel wood cube in an eleven-voxel grid with an air collar. A one-voxel spherical edit heats wood from 310 to 790 K. The positive-conduction cases use the base Playground grid spacing of 4 cm, conductivity 0.15 W/(m K), density 16 kg/m³, thermal diffusivity 5.859375 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s, and one wood substep per 1/120 s gas step. We ran the **isolated wood recurrence** for 1,440 steps, or 12 seconds including the preset warm-up. The uniform cold trajectory is an exact fixed point. The calculation uses fixed, equal wood masses and no imposed surface flux. It omits the gas dynamics, surface reaction, intervening cooling and image formation. The non-physical binary64 replay shows how rounding can affect the boundary. ### Why a sub-ignition exposed edit has a different contract

At an ambient open face with zero water and both wood temperatures between 293.15 and 450 K, fuel release can be zero on both branches. The heat transfer still changes. Writing h for the exchange coefficient, the native lower clamp gives a transfer of −min(h dx² dt, m × 1600 / 8) (Twood − 293.15) when Tgas is ambient. For positive coefficients this is strictly temperature-dependent before rounding. At a hot face the upper clamp instead depends only on gas capacity and temperature. Two distinct material states could agree there, but every incident face, mass contribution and fuel/enthalpy term would need a cheap live test. This screen measured no such hot-face interval. ### The next admission test

The base Firewoods preset supplies no positive conductivity. Playground explicitly enables solid conduction and the registration uses the released combustible label, Gaussian-to-voxel mapping, separate obstacle voxels and the fitted ground. The Bonsai material is an authored ornament ROI with its own transform, obstacle voxels and fitted ground settings. A standalone Gaussian PLY or a FieryGS wood mask alone does not establish the correct open-gas boundary. Upstream also has conservation and disconnected-component tests of this diffusion operator. A finite-speed stencil alone is a standard dependence property. One bounded follow-up can qualify a **researcher-defined core-preheat comparison on Playground**. Use exactly the base preset, original registered wood and obstacle masks, calibrated scene, default dry time interval and the source camera target [0.05, 0.2, 0.15]. Compute a single geometric clearance map before any treatment. Fix the center to the wood voxel nearest that target in scene coordinates (lexicographic grid index for a tie), then compare the same radius 1.1 dx with two distinct core temperatures, 620 and 790 K, under otherwise identical localized initialization. Keep the prescribed center even if the brush is exposed. Give the preflight one host process and 120 s after verified assets are staged. Record the full changed footprint, exact gas-seed equality and distances to all open faces. Stop on missing registration, a seed change, disconnected support, or a static bound of at most the one scheduling lag. If it passes, the next cost decision still requires meaningful later surface/gas or image differences on *this same edit*, A/A bit reproducibility, and a declared shot deliverable before timing. The prescribed temperatures and shot study are investigator choices.

The ordinary baseline also gets the same static distance proof and compiled scene whenever applicable. Any stronger method must demonstrate additional savings over these controls.
