# Reusing simulation work after two alternatives diverge

Twigg and James already reuse motion after an animation edit has changed some objects. Hybinette and Fujimoto already share component state across distinct, interacting simulation versions. These are direct precedents for the broad reuse claim. The question specific to T117/T118 is whether a later checkpoint from one nested brush edit, together with the original edits, suffices to construct the other edit's complete native physical state before two-way feedback distinguishes their gas.

| Primary publication | What is reused and how the simulation continues |
|---|---|
| Christopher D. Twigg and Doug L. James, *Many-Worlds Browsing for Control of Multibody Dynamics*, SIGGRAPH 2007, §5, Figs. 6–7 | After selecting an example and edit time, the system advances changed rigid bodies while unaffected bodies follow stored trajectories. A new interaction activates additional bodies. Ghosts following the original paths also detect a contact removed by the edit. Reactivated bodies participate in dynamics. This is post-divergence reuse in an actual animation system. The authors enlarge ghost geometry to account for quantized stored motion; their criterion is physically valid refinement. [Authors' paper, §5](https://graphics.cs.cmu.edu/projects/mwb/manyWorldsBrowsing.pdf) |
| Maria Hybinette and Richard M. Fujimoto, *Cloning Parallel Simulations*, TOMACS 2001, §§4–5 | Logical processes from different versions can share a single physical process even after other processes have diverged. Incoming messages determine when to duplicate messages or split the receiving process. This preserves the distinct simulation executions in a discrete-event model where all cross-process effects are explicit messages. It directly anticipates sharing independent state and allowing divergent components to interact later. [Publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/508366.508370) · [Paper, §§4–5](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=a445d687dbdfd1e6394c54c343982a948ccc5f10&repid=rep1&type=pdf) |
| Morten Bojsen-Hansen and Chris Wojtan, *Generalized Non-reflecting Boundaries for Fluid Re-simulation*, SIGGRAPH 2016 | A changed fluid region is recomputed using prescribed space- and time-varying exterior flow. Absorbing layers suppress outgoing reflections, enabling local geometry edits and higher-resolution detail to blend into an existing take. The prescribed exterior is a different continuation contract from two free-running coupled branches matching their independent native replays. [Authors' project and paper](https://alas.dk/publications/2016/GNRBfFRS/) |
| Purvi Goel and Doug L. James, *Unified Many-Worlds Browsing of Arbitrary Physics-Based Animations*, SIGGRAPH 2022 | WorldPack stores compressed volumetric observations of simulated alternatives for fast approximate spacetime queries, browsing and rendering. It provides a graphics precedent for choosing a world through a spatial query. The published WorldPack query representation carries occupancy and other low-fidelity fields; it does not supply a complete native physical checkpoint for a different world. [Authors' project, paper and format](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/umwb/) |

**Manuscript scope.** Attribute selective trajectory reuse and state sharing across divergent versions to prior work. T117 derives a finite interval from the actual solver's graph-donor prefix, emission cursor and brush geometry. T118 takes ordered gas particles and the RNG position at the end of that interval from the light sibling, evolves the extra directly painted sites under the native rounded updates, reconstructs the full sibling's ordered frontier, and resumes feedback. Its two development checks compare the resulting physical state and successors with independent full-brush replays. Report the operation, solver conditions, and measured benefit against a full simulator given the same transport opportunity.
