# A full fitted room exposed an error in the proposed brush picker

Two fixed screen locations on the previously unused 30,000-iteration Bonsai fit both selected Gaussian **867267**. The test stopped before simulation under its original rule for coincident primitives. The two source rays are distinct. The projected primitive chosen by the opacity calculation would lie entirely outside the native rendered viewport, even with the most generous quad extent allowed by the shader. This pinpoints a failure of using the unconstrained projected-opacity calculation from the foreground experiments to place brushes in a full fitted room. The primitive has camera-space coordinates (20.8753, 0.5758, 0.000171239) in original fit units. Its three Gaussian standard deviations are 0.067380, 0.016733 and 0.013078, and its intrinsic opacity is 0.999316. The near-zero positive forward depth and large off-axis center make the unrestricted perspective Jacobian give it a 3-sigma major radius of about 108 billion quarter-resolution pixels. Its mean is approximately 98.28 million pixels to the right of the viewport. Both rays fall under the resulting long ellipse. At their common depth the actual back-projected points differ by 0.0000115824 fit units and round-trip to their registered positions within 0.000001 pixel. The picker uses the Gaussian's center as the native brush site, causing the duplicate. [Projection and opacity implementation](../t107-visible-burn-progression/measure.py) · [Computation for this scene](prepare.py)

The source camera matrix maps camera axes to the reconstruction. T42's native camera adapter applies the same camera-center subtraction and right/down/forward convention before conversion to OpenGL. The native shader clamps the off-axis Jacobian and caps each of the two footprint radii at half the longest viewport side. Even a square corner can move horizontally by at most 551.55 quarter-resolution pixels here. This upper bound alone excludes the selected splat from both image positions. The native shader's 0.2 near rejection uses **world** units. The unexecuted recipe would put this exceptionally shallow splat at world depth 5.168, so the quantitative diagnosis uses the invariant image-space radius bound. This conclusion follows from the source geometry and shader. No native pixels were acquired. ## Source asset and verification

The release provided 1,244,819 Gaussians with 62 float32 attributes apiece and 292 camera rows. We registered views 0 and 146 and read the entire fitted PLY in original order. Sixty-nine validated byte-range responses totaling 260,771,299 bytes delivered the archive tail, member headers, camera table and compressed PLY, with no retry. Acquisition took 105.70 seconds. The decompressed PLY was 308,716,644 bytes. The receipt contains the two CRC32 and SHA-256 identities. Third-party asset redistribution terms were not determined by this experiment; fetch the files from the original distributor and keep local copies in the ignored directory. [Inria pretrained models](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting#3d-gaussian-splatting-for-real-time-radiance-field-rendering) · [Camera and PLY acquisition receipts](ACQUISITION.json) · [Earlier Bonsai observation archive metadata audit](../t28-bounded-carrier-decision/README.md)

From this folder, `python verify.py` checks the stop, contributor values and native footprint bound from the public receipts with standard Python. For an independent recomputation, run `python acquire.py`, then `python prepare.py --check` using Python 3.11+ with NumPy and Pillow. The second command checks the entire acquired Gaussian set, evaluates the frozen opacity rule and compares all derived values with the published JSON. Geometry remains in `local/`. The selection code also implements how a valid result would configure the original scene and source-camera adapter. Another shot would need a separately registered picker with native near/footprint/ordering handling and validation against the actual viewed image. The two Bonsai positions stay closed under the registered definition.
