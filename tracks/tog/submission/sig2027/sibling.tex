\subsection{Transporting a checkpoint between brush alternatives}
A nested pair of brush edits differs initially on a set $D$ of extra painted sites, disjoint from the measured shell. Apply the service test to the \emph{common pre-click} frontier. Until its first possible wrap, all serviced graph and gas sources come from this parent. In each branch the sources see the same native gas and random draws. At every site outside $D$ all material inputs, thresholds and rounded clocks therefore agree. The light branch's newly ignited IDs, in native order with IDs in $D$ removed, are exactly the full branch's new ignitions.

At the boundary copy the light branch's physical state. For each extra painted site reconstruct the full brush's already-burning state from its pre-click values, using native heat decay and combustion clocks. Set the frontier to the ordered common parent, the full brush additions in their own order, then the light branch's filtered subsequent births. Keep the particle arrays, random position and cursor. This constructs the full branch without its future trajectory.

The shared prefix lasted 24 steps on the woodpile and 16 on Playground, with 214 and 1,317 extra sites. Every physical field matched native replay at the boundary, the next step, $+44$ and $+90$ in both scenes. Neither light branch naturally ignited a site in $D$ before its boundary. All per-frame ignition IDs agreed through the deadlines. The primary woodpile choice used 90 light and 44 full steps, so 24 of 134 physical updates could be shared. Playground skipped the second stroke, making a speculative checkpoint overhead on all three operations. These measurements concern the same two developer-defined actions.
