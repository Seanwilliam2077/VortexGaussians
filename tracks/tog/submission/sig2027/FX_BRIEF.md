# A shot brief from an independent FX artist

This sheet is ready for a future session. No artist has completed it. The observer has two roles: record a real production decision in the artist's own words, then test whether the experimental tool helps them make and continue that edit. Use an unopened shot or edit if the artist has already seen this project's example results.

## Before viewing simulation outcomes

Give the artist a neutral starting view or clean plate, camera controls, and the available interventions. Keep the research team's previous choices, thresholds, predictions and timing results hidden. Invite the artist to decline if none of the offered shots or edits matters to their work.

1. Describe the intended shot and the specific decision you would use this tool to make.
2. Which part of the scene, which camera and which frame or time interval would you inspect? Describe in your own words what would convince you to keep a take.
3. What would make you revise or discard every option? If several takes work, how would you choose?
4. What do you need to receive after choosing a take, and what would you do with it next?

Record the exact answers before showing alternatives. With the artist, write a testable version of their criterion and confirm that it preserves their intent. Record any remaining ambiguity. Store the original brief privately and timestamp its digest. A brief that depends on gas appearance, aesthetics, or a criterion the current method cannot answer remains in the record with that disposition.

## Decision and independent playback

Run the agreed edit with a fixed camera and deadline. Record the user's choice, confidence, need for additional information, and any refusal to choose. Give the artist anonymized, randomly ordered full native playbacks of all alternatives, including takes the tool would have skipped. Hide method names, results and timings during playback. Ask which alternatives fulfill the frozen brief, whether the initial choice still stands, and which part of the playback decided it. The artist may accept several or none.

Measure from issuing the edit to the requested deliverable. For a take the artist wants to continue, include restoration and the first correct successor frame. Record setup separately. Compare to a matched ordinary replay that can stop early, share cameras, and reuse identical pre-click geometry and ordinary caches. Report the wall time for the tool separately from the artist's inspection time. Preserve changes of mind, disagreements between numerical criteria and playback, unusable criteria, and ordinary wins.

This is a single-case test of relevance and feasibility. Publish only permissioned, de-identified observations. A laboratory timing comparison or a response generated by another agent does not count as a completed artist session.
