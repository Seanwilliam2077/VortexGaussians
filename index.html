Vortex Gaussians is a browser prototype for vortex-driven fire and smoke rendered as Gaussian splats. The dynamic effects are depth-interleaved with a Gaussian-splat environment, allowing approximate per-primitive occlusion in both directions. The default layered visual model and the separate 3D research paths have different physical and numerical contracts.

Abstract

The default layered mode maps vortex-particle positions and visual attributes to Gaussian render primitives: temperature controls emissive color, density controls opacity, and velocity gradients drive an anisotropic covariance. This shared storage is an implementation choice; simulation kernels and rendering covariances are not asserted to define the same field. The layered VPM uses regularized vortex-kernel evaluations and a Barnes–Hut approximation, without a fluid grid or pressure projection in that mode. The VIC comparison uses grids. Optional inter-slice vorticity exchange is disabled by default and does not reproduce full 3D transport or vortex stretching. A depth-interleaved emission–absorption rasterizer combines flame, smoke, embers and reconstructed 3DGS scenes. The author historically reported 17.1 ms per frame (58 FPS) for the layered browser preset, 9 layers × 600 particles, at 1280×800 on an RTX 3080 with CPU JavaScript simulation. This measurement was not rerun in the current audit. Replay checks compare simulation-state hashes under fixed code, inputs, configuration and runtime; they do not make timing or cross-platform results bit-reproducible. The separate numerical audits do not establish physical accuracy or a new method advantage for the complete fire system.

Heuristic fire spread on captured scenes

In the layered demo, heuristic heat, fuel, char and glow states live directly on the reconstruction's own splats. The flame ignites nearby geometry, fire spreads splat-to-splat on a heat threshold, charring is written into the splats' albedo, and burning geometry re-seeds the solver with new plumes. These controls are not calibrated material properties. The separate fixed-parcel 3D thermal tests do not validate this combustion model.

Out of the browser: an outdoor capture inside NVIDIA Omniverse

An author-provided offline demonstration of a dependency-free C++17 implementation, run headless, burning a drone reconstruction of a village inside NVIDIA Omniverse (Isaac Sim 6.0). The reconstruction, the flame, the smoke and the char are one primitive type — OpenUSD 26.03's ParticleField3DGaussianSplat — ray-traced in a single pass. The capture holds 8,362,588 Gaussians; the shot renders a full-density crop of 1,892,830 static surroundings plus the 736,360 that make up the farmhouse, which is split out because it is the part that changes. 220,706 splats burn at the peak and 400,000 are charred by the last frame — 54% of its reported splat count. The current browser audit did not verify this separate implementation or rerun the clip.

GS-native composition: the fire lives inside the scene

The fire is a second stream of Gaussians merged into the scene's own depth order, not a layer drawn over it. Loaded reconstructions are interleaved with the flame by an asymmetric two-stream sort — the small dynamic set exactly, the scene counting-sorted into 1536 view-depth buckets in O(m+K), so scene ordering is approximate. Historical author-reported overheads were 22.9 vs 21.2 ms median render at 45.8k scene splats (+8%) and 291.0 vs 239.3 ms at 729,826 (+22%). These measurements were not rerun in the current audit; see the historical manuscript for their configuration.

Default layered pipeline: particle-to-splat mapping

A directable coarse field — the top levels of the solver's own Barnes–Hut tree — drives N per-slice grid-free Gaussian vortex-particle simulations. Each slice exposes its particles through a minimal contract {x, T, d, u}; the renderer maps each particle to an emissive anisotropic Gaussian and rasterizes all of them — fire, smoke, embers, and the background 3DGS scene — in one depth-sorted emission–absorption pass. This mode does not voxelize the flow for rendering; alternative solver and research paths use different representations and mappings.

Related tasks and current evidence

"GS-native" means the phenomenon is rendered as Gaussian primitives depth-interleaved with the scene. This describes a rendering interface; it does not establish physical equivalence, priority, or an advantage from sharing every simulation and display sample. The following primary sources address overlapping but different tasks.

Primary sourceTask and relevant overlap
PhysGaussian (CVPR 2024)MPM-based material dynamics integrated with Gaussian simulation and rendering representations.
FieryGS (ICLR 2026)Scene material reasoning, volumetric combustion and rendering integrated with reconstructed GS environments.
Gaussians on Fire (ECCV 2026)High-frequency reconstruction of observed flames; a different task from forward fire synthesis.
LagrangianSplats (SIGGRAPH 2026)Divergence-free transport of Gaussian primitives for fluid reconstruction.
Vortex Gaussians (this prototype)Layered vortex-driven Gaussian effects, heuristic scene spread, and separately audited numerical and architecture experiments. A distinct method advantage remains unestablished.

Historical timing context: the manuscript quotes FieryGS at 2.37 s/frame (RTX 4090D), Gaussian Fluids at 38–228 s per time step (RTX 4090), and production layered fire at about 20 s per slice per frame on a GPU farm. It reports this prototype's layered browser preset at 17.1 ms/frame (58 FPS, five-seed median; RTX 3080, 1280×800, 9 × 600 particles, single-threaded CPU-JS simulation). These figures concern different tasks, physical models, resolutions and hardware. They are retained as historical reports, not a matched-quality performance comparison; the current audit did not rerun these measurements.

Current evidence distinguishes finite-capacity 3D thermal coupling (P4b), a native thermal-query repair (P5b), and an isolated standard physical reference (P5c). In the separate P7b prescribed-motion thermal task, 2048 independent thermal states and 8192 shared states both met the same 2% response/display tolerances. The proxy had a median paired CPU-to-display-temperature-buffer time ratio of 0.2730 and lower accounted array storage. That screen does not measure a renderer, VPM, or full-frame speedup. See Evidence status for scope, provenance and limitations.
