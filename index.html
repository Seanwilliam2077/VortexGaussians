Using nothing but a set of Lagrangian vortex particles, Vortex Gaussians generates fire and smoke by forward physical simulation in real time and renders them as native Gaussian-splatting content: the fire's Gaussians share one global depth order and one emission–absorption blend state with a Gaussian-splat environment, so a foreground scene object correctly occludes the flame and the flame occludes the scene behind it.

Abstract

Fire and smoke remain absent from Gaussian-splatting (GS) scenes: physics-integrated GS handles solids and liquids, GS fire methods only reconstruct observed flames, and the one system that simulates new fire composites ray-marched volumes over an opaque backdrop at seconds per frame. We present the first system that generates fire and smoke by forward physical simulation in real time and renders them as native Gaussian-splatting content. Our key insight is that Lagrangian vortex particles and 3D Gaussians are the same class of point primitive: one particle set serves simultaneously as simulation state and render primitive—position to Gaussian mean, temperature to emissive blackbody color, density to opacity, velocity gradient to anisotropic covariance—with no voxelization, meshing, or simulation-to-renderer conversion anywhere. A grid-free Gaussian vortex-particle solver drives them — each splat is a Lamb–Oseen vortex element, velocity and temperature are closed-form evaluations of the rendered mixture under a Barnes–Hut treecode, with no Poisson solve and no grid anywhere — with a new bidirectional inter-slice vorticity-coupling operator that restores the out-of-plane transport stacking drops; one depth-sorted emission–absorption rasterizer composites flame, smoke, embers, and reconstructed 3DGS scenes with per-primitive mutual occlusion, so a foreground object correctly occludes the flame. Our single-file WebGL2 prototype runs in real time on a workstation-class desktop — 17.1 ms per frame (58 FPS) at the shipping preset, scaling near-linearly in slice count — supporting interactive in-scene ignition, wind, and coupling control, with a deterministic-replay harness that makes every reported number bit-reproducible.

How it works: one particle set, zero conversion

A directable coarse field — the top levels of the solver’s own Barnes–Hut tree — drives N per-slice 2D grid-free Gaussian vortex-particle simulations. Each slice exposes its particles through a minimal contract {x, T, d, u}; the renderer lifts every particle directly into an emissive anisotropic Gaussian and rasterizes all Gaussians — fire, smoke, embers, and the background 3DGS scene — in one depth-sorted emission–absorption pass with HDR post-processing. There is no voxelization or resampling anywhere in the pipeline.

GS-native composition: the fire lives inside the scene

Loaded 3DGS reconstructions are merged into the same global depth order as the fire by an asymmetric two-stream interleave: the small dynamic set is sorted exactly, the scene is counting-sorted into 96 view-depth buckets in O(m+K), and the two pre-ordered streams are interleaved near-to-far under one blend state — at no measured overhead (3.5 vs 3.6 ms/frame at 3.2k scene splats; 13.0 vs 13.3 ms at 79k). Drag the slider to compare against a fair baseline: on the right the scene is sorted correctly among itself and only the interleave between the two streams is removed — the structure of compositing a ray-marched volume onto a reconstructed backdrop. The scene renders faithfully, yet the flame sits in front of every primitive: the rear post stops occluding it and its base spills past the near edge of the floor. The scene itself burns: fire spread on the splats

Combustion state — heat, fuel, char, glow — lives directly on the reconstruction's splats. The simulated flame ignites nearby geometry; fire spreads splat-to-splat with conventional heat-threshold physics (after Pirk et al. 2017, Hädrich et al. 2021); charring is written straight into the splats' albedo and opacity; and burning geometry re-seeds the solver with new plumes — a real-time, bidirectional simulation↔scene loop. Below: a single ignition inside the fire ring spreads through the fuel, the splats themselves glow and char, and the plume above the fire is fed by the burning scene rather than only by the original emitter.

Two orthogonal coupling mechanisms

A coarse global field nudges every slice toward one bulk motion (tunable c ∈ [0,1]); a new bidirectional inter-slice vorticity exchange ωk += Δt·κz(ωk−1+ωk+1−2ωk) reconstructs the out-of-plane transport ∂²ω/∂z² that stacked-2D models structurally drop — circulation-conserving by construction, and the first slice-to-slice communication in layered fire (production layered fire has none). Quantifying its statistical effect turned out to be subtle: under our deterministic-replay protocol, snapshot field-correlation probes are dominated by the structural correlation the shared source already induces, so the paper reports this as a deliberate negative result and defers quantitative validation to an energy-spectrum comparison against a true-3D reference.

View-aligned slices survive any viewpoint

Because simulation state lives on particles — not slice rasters — slices re-orient with the camera at zero re-simulation cost. A headless probe confirms viewpoint isotropy (peak brightness 758/758 side/front); pinning slices to the world instead collapses the volume into stripes when seen edge-on (531).

Flow-driven Gaussians: the fluid shears the splats

Each Gaussian's covariance is evolved by the local velocity gradient, Σ̇ = LΣ + ΣL⊤, with determinant renormalization and an aspect clamp acting on the rendered quantity itself — the rest-configuration-free counterpart of PhysGaussian's FΣ₀F⊤, immune to the classic "sliver" blow-up under sustained shear. An optional trajectory-filament stretch elongates fast particles into path-aligned streaks.
